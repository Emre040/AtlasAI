**Q:** Which enhanced antibody validation methods does the Human Protein Atlas apply for Western blot, and for immunohistochemistry what does orthogonal validation compare the antibody staining against and across how many normal tissues?

For Western blot, the Human Protein Atlas applies five enhanced antibody validation strategies: genetic validation, recombinant expression validation, independent antibody validation, orthogonal validation, and capture MS validation [1]; these enhanced validation principles are based on the five "pillars" of the International Working Group for Antibody Validation (IWGAV) and antibodies that fulfil the criteria are labelled "Enhanced" [2]. For immunohistochemistry, orthogonal validation compares the antibody staining against an antibody-independent method, specifically it "is based on manual evaluation of the correlation between the staining intensity of a single-target antibody and corresponding mRNA levels across up to 46 normal tissues" [3], with the antibody receiving the "Enhanced" score if the difference in mRNA levels between the two representative tissues is at least 4-fold [4].
