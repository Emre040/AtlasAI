## Proteomic association vs. causation

These results are **associations, not causal claims.** The HPA blood proximity‑extension analyses compare measured plasma protein levels between each disease cohort and healthy controls; a differential logFC (and even a large |ΔlogFC| between the two diseases) reflects only a statistical difference in circulating abundance, not that the protein causes RA or SLE, nor that the two diseases differ mechanistically at that gene. Several caveats reinforce this: the cohort is modest (1,165 measured proteins), direction discordance can arise from treatment effects, comorbidity, cohort composition, or assay/p‑value noise rather than biology, and cross‑disease λ comparisons mix disease effect with each cohort's technical variance. The strong overall correlation (ρ ≈ 0.76) is consistent with a shared, largely concordant plasma‑protein response in these two autoimmune diseases, but establishing causality would require independent replication, directional Mendelian randomization, or functional/experimental validation — none of which is provided by the HPA differential‑expression data alone. The secretome/blood‑concentration annotations (e.g., IL6, CXCL13, PPY detectable by immunoassay; IL6, MMP7, VSIG4, SULT2A1 by mass spectrometry) only describe whether a protein is measurable in blood, which is distinct from it being a causal driver.
