**Q:** Which enhanced antibody validation methods does the Human Protein Atlas apply for Western blot, and for immunohistochemistry what does orthogonal validation compare the antibody staining against and across how many normal tissues?

## Answer

**Enhanced antibody validation methods for Western blot**

The Human Protein Atlas applies **five** enhanced validation strategies for Western blot:
1. **Genetic validation** (siRNA knock-down/knock-out)
2. **Recombinant expression validation** (over-expression)
3. **Independent antibody validation** (two antibodies with non-overlapping epitopes)
4. **Orthogonal validation** (comparison against mass spectrometry–quantified protein levels and/or RNA)
5. **Capture MS validation** (comparing the band's molecular weight against a capture-MS protein size)

The page states: *"Western blot: Genetic validation, Recombinant expression validation, Independent antibody validation, Orthogonal validation, Capture MS validation."*

**IHC orthogonal validation**

For immunohistochemistry, orthogonal validation compares the **staining intensity of a single-target antibody against the corresponding mRNA levels** (an antibody-independent method). This comparison is done **across up to 46 normal tissues**. An antibody receives the "Enhanced" score if consistency between staining intensity and mRNA levels is high or medium and the difference in mRNA levels between the two selected representative tissues is at least 4-fold.

The page states: *"Orthogonal validation is based on manual evaluation of the correlation between the staining intensity of a single-target antibody and corresponding mRNA levels across up to 46 normal tissues."*

Source (both statements): https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/tissue/method/antibody+validation

Note: the same page separately describes IHC *standard* validation as using "44 normal tissues," whereas the IHC *orthogonal (enhanced)* validation explicitly says "up to 46 normal tissues" — I've reported each as stated.
