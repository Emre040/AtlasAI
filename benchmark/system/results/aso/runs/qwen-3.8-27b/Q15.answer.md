**Findings**

- Among 316 proteins with blood concentrations measured by both immunoassay and mass spectrometry, 93 are tissue-enriched in liver and 223 are not. (evidence: a24 row 0: count=93, group=Liver-enriched; row 1: count=223, group=Rest)
- The median log10 mass spectrometry blood concentration is 9.996 for liver-enriched proteins and 6.699 for the rest, a difference of 3.297 log10 units, corresponding to a 1,980-fold higher median MS concentration in the liver-enriched group. (evidence: a24 row 0: median_log10_ms=9.99564, diff_log10_ms=3.29667, fold_change=1980.02, group=Liver-enriched; row 1: median_log10_ms=6.69897, diff_log10_ms=-3.29667, fold_change=1980.02, group=Rest)

**Limitations**

- Concentrations are reported in pg/L as stored in the atlas; the question phrased them in ng/mL (1 ng/mL = 1000 pg/L). (1000: not verified against the data of this study)
- The liver-enriched classification is based on RNA-level tissue enrichment (≥4-fold higher mRNA in liver vs any other tissue), not on protein-level enrichment.
- Median is used to summarise per-gene concentrations when multiple measurements exist; the distribution shape within each group is not characterised beyond the median.
- The log10 ratio (IA/MS) reflects the relative magnitude of the two measurement methods for each protein and is not a biological ratio.
