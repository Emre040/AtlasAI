- These results reflect differential protein abundance measured in blood by proximity-extension assay in a cross-sectional cohort design. They establish statistical association between disease status and protein level, not causation: the observed changes may be consequences of disease (e.g., inflammatory cytokine release, tissue damage), markers of disease activity, or contributors to pathogenesis. Direction of effect and temporal ordering cannot be inferred from this data. (evidence: a10 row 0: x=logFC, y=logFC_2, method=spearman, n=1165, r=0.76, p_value=6.33e-220)

**Limitations**

- The PEX data are cross-sectional: protein abundance is measured once per subject, so temporal dynamics and direction of causation cannot be established.
- The common universe (1,165 genes) is restricted to genes with finite values in both comparisons; genes with missing or non-finite values in either disease are excluded and may include biologically relevant signals.
- The top-12 list is drawn from 219 genes significant in both diseases; it captures the largest between-disease discordance but does not represent the full set of shared or disease-specific signals.
- Blood immunoassay and mass-spectrometry coverage is sparse (4 of 12 genes each); absence of a measurement does not imply the protein is absent from blood.
- The secretome annotation reflects protein classification in the Human Protein Atlas and does not confirm functional secretion in the disease context.
- No multiple-testing correction beyond the atlas-reported adjusted p-values is applied to the secondary analyses (correlation, ranking).
