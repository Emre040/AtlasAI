### Reasons and Tool Limitations

1. **Failure of Prognostic Query Filters**:
   - The `search_cancer_markers` endpoint returns empty sets (`[]`) when filtered by `prognostic="favorable"` or `prognostic="unfavorable"`. Without the prognostic filter, it performs a general keyword match on gene names/descriptions rather than returning verified TCGA prognostic gene sets.
   - The `advanced_search` endpoint similarly returns empty sets when queried with `cancerPrognostic` filters.

2. **Incomplete Cancer Cohort Coverage in Pathology Data**:
   - The gene-level pathology endpoint (`get_pathology_data`) only returns TCGA survival data for **4 cancer types**:
     - Breast Invasive Carcinoma (TCGA)
     - Colon Adenocarcinoma (TCGA)
     - Lung Adenocarcinoma (TCGA)
     - Prostate Adenocarcinoma (TCGA)
   - The remaining **8 cohorts** (*liver hepatocellular carcinoma, kidney renal clear cell carcinoma, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, stomach adenocarcinoma, ovary serous cystadenocarcinoma, thyroid carcinoma, glioblastoma multiforme, and testicular germ cell tumor*) are not available in the API's pathology data.
   - Even for the 4 available cancer types, data can only be queried one gene at a time, precluding genome-wide aggregation across all ~20,000 human protein-coding genes.

3. **Origin of This Specific Dataset**:
   - This exact analysis—evaluating genome-wide **validated favourable** vs. **validated unfavourable** prognostic genes across these 12 specific TCGA cohorts and cross-referencing them against genes **tissue enriched** in the corresponding tissue of origin—originates from the landmark Human Protein Atlas Pathology Atlas publication:
     > **Uhlén et al.**, *"A pathology atlas of the human cancer transcriptome"*, ***Science* 357**, eaan2507 (2017) (specifically Figure 2B–C and Supplementary Table S4).
   - While these summary statistics were published as part of the Pathology Atlas study, the current tool endpoints do not expose cohort-level summary counts or full cohort-wide prognostic classification tables.
