### Methodological Distinction: Proteomic Association vs. Causation

When analyzing plasma proximity-extension proteomics in autoimmune diseases (such as RA and SLE):

* **Proteomic Association**:
  - Measures statistical correlation or differences in circulating protein abundance (NPX / logFC) between disease cases and healthy controls.
  - Associations frequently reflect downstream secondary consequences of disease processes (e.g., systemic inflammation, tissue injury, cytokine release syndromes), acute-phase responses, altered renal clearance, or confounding factors (e.g., immunosuppressive therapy, age, sex, BMI, sample handling).
* **Causation**:
  - Indicates that an altered circulating level or function of the protein plays an etiologic, mechanistic, or driver role in disease pathogenesis.
  - Establishing causality requires orthogonal evidence, such as:
    - **Mendelian Randomization (MR)**: Using cis-protein quantitative trait loci (cis-pQTLs) as genetic instrumental variables to assess whether genetically predicted protein levels alter disease risk without reverse causation or horizontal pleiotropy.
    - **Functional & Mechanistic Validation**: In vitro and in vivo genetic perturbation (knockout/overexpression) assays demonstrating biological mechanisms directly mediating disease pathology.
