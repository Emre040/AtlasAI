1. **Chromosome subject of the pilot:** **Human chromosome 22** — the smallest human chromosome (≈50 Mb, ~200 protein-coding genes). It was chosen precisely because it made a manageable proof-of-concept pilot, called the **"Human Chromosome 22 Proteome"** project, before the atlas was expanded to the whole genome.

2. **When the pilot began:** **2003**, launched at the Proteomics Science Park (Sci-technova) associated with Uppsala University after the funding proposal was submitted in 2002 (Swedish Research Council backing).

3. **The question in the 2002 funding proposal:** The founders' core question was whether the **protein expression patterns of the genes on human chromosome 22 could be systematically mapped across normal human tissues** (i.e., in which tissues each chromosome 22 protein is expressed, and at what level, with its subcellular localization) — essentially asking *what is the protein-level counterpart of the chromosome's gene expression in the normal body*, in the hope that success at this small scale would justify atlas-wide mapping of all human protein expression.
