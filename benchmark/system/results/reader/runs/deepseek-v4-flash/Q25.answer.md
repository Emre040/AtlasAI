**Q:** Which enhanced antibody validation methods does the Human Protein Atlas apply for Western blot, and for immunohistochemistry what does orthogonal validation compare the antibody staining against and across how many normal tissues?

For Western blot, the Human Protein Atlas applies five enhanced validation strategies: genetic validation, recombinant expression validation, independent antibody validation, orthogonal validation and Capture MS validation [1]. For immunohistochemistry, orthogonal validation rests on manual evaluation of the correlation between the staining intensity of a single-target antibody and the corresponding mRNA levels, and it does this across up to 46 normal tissues [2]; an antibody is scored "Enhanced" when the staining/mRNA consistency is high or medium and the two representative tissues selected differ by at least 4-fold in mRNA levels [3].

Sources:
[1] "Western blot: Genetic validation, Recombinant expression validation, Independent antibody validation, Orthogonal validation, Capture MS validation" — The human proteome - Methods summary - The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/tissue/method/antibody+validation)
[2] "Orthogonal validation is based on manual evaluation of the correlation between the staining intensity of a single-target antibody and corresponding mRNA levels across up to 46 normal tissues." — The human proteome - Methods summary - The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/tissue/method/antibody+validation)
[3] "If the difference in mRNA levels between the two representative tissues is at least 4-fold, the antibody will receive the validation score "Enhanced"." — The human proteome - Methods summary - The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/tissue/method/antibody+validation)
