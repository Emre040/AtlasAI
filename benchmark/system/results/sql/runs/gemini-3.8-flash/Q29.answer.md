### 6. Distinguishing Proteomic Association from Causation

1. **Association in Blood PEA Data:**
   - The measured differential abundance values reflect **statistical associations** between circulating protein concentrations and disease status at the time of sampling.
   - Circulating protein levels can change due to:
     - **Reactive processes:** Acute-phase responses (e.g., IL6, DEFA1) triggered by downstream systemic inflammation.
     - **Cellular leakage and tissue remodeling:** Cell death, membrane turnover, or protease release from inflamed synovium or kidneys (e.g., MMP7).
     - **Altered clearance or binding:** Changes in renal filtration, liver clearance, or decoy receptor buffering during active autoimmune episodes.
2. **Causal Inference Considerations:**
   - An elevated circulating protein is not necessarily an etiological driver; it may be an innocent bystander, downstream symptom, or a protective negative-feedback mediator.
   - To establish **causality**, orthogonal genetic and mechanistic approaches are required:
     - **Mendelian Randomization (MR):** Using protein quantitative trait loci (pQTLs) as instrumental variables to test whether genetically predicted protein abundance causally affects disease risk.
     - **Longitudinal and Interventional Studies:** Assessing whether protein level changes precede disease onset and whether targeted inhibition (e.g., anti-IL-6R biologics) modulates disease activity.
