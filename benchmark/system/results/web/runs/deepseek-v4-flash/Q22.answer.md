## Current release of the Human Protein Atlas

**Version 25.1**, released **May 25, 2026** (Ensembl version 109). This is the latest entry on the Release History page: https://www.proteinatlas.org/about/releases

A corresponding press release dated May 28, 2026, is on the Press Room page: https://www.proteinatlas.org/news/press+room

## What new data it added

According to the release notes, version 25.1 focused on integrating mass-spectrometry protein data (https://www.proteinatlas.org/about/releases):

- **Single Cell resource — Deep Visual Proteomics (DVP):** Cell-type-resolved MS-based protein profiles across 27 different cell types representing 14 different tissues were integrated. Selected Single Cell Type data were matched to the DVP samples for side-by-side RNA vs. protein comparison, with specificity overlap and agreement scores. A total of 27 cell-type shapes (3–5 replicates each), grouped into 24 cell types for classifications, are included.

- **Tissue resource — quantitative proteomics:** MS-based quantitative proteomics profiles for bulk tissue samples were integrated; 20 tissue types overlapping with the DVP samples were added to gene-summary pages, providing protein profiles for **over 12,700 genes**.

The news article on this release adds that 27 cell types across 14 tissues from a single healthy female donor were analyzed with DVP, detecting **almost 14,000 proteins** (https://www.proteinatlas.org/news/2026-05-28/hpa-integrates-cell-type-resolution-protein-data-based-on-deep-visual-proteomics).

## Antibodies and human genes covered

The version **25.1** release notes do **not** state a top-line antibody/gene count. The most recent figures given on the site come from the **version 25.0** release notes (November 11, 2025), which describe it as "the twenty-fifth major release of the Human Protein Atlas with **over 27,800 antibodies**, targeting proteins from **over 17,400 human genes (~88% of the human protein-coding genes)**." https://www.proteinatlas.org/about/releases

So for the current release (25.1), I can confirm the new data added and the release date/version, but the antibody and gene counts are the ones stated for v25.0 (over 27,800 antibodies; over 17,400 genes, ~88%), since no separate figure is given specifically for 25.1.

Note: The current About page (https://www.proteinatlas.org/about) still describes the atlas as consisting of nine resources and gives resource-level coverage numbers rather than a single atlas-wide antibody count — e.g., the Tissue resource's protein data covers 15,312 genes (76%) with available antibodies, and the Subcellular resource covers 13,603 genes (67%) directly plus predictions for a further 3,459, totaling 17,062 genes (85%).
