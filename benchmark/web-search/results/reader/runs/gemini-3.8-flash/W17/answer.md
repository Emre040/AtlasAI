**Q:** In the cancer section, what p-value must the Kaplan-Meier analysis reach for a gene to be considered prognostic, and what distinguishes an unfavorable prognostic gene from a favorable one?

In the cancer section, a gene is defined as prognostic if its log-rank P value is less than 0.001 in maximally separated Kaplan-Meier analysis [1]. It is designated as an unfavorable prognostic gene if the group of patients with high expression has a higher observed event rate than expected; otherwise, it is classified as a favorable prognostic gene [2].

Sources:
[1] "genes with log rank P values less than 0.001 in maximally separated Kaplan-Meier analysis were defined as prognostic genes" — The human cancer proteome - Methods summary - The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/cancer/method)
[2] "If the group of patients with high expression of a selected prognostic gene has a higher observed event than expected event, it is an unfavorable prognostic gene; otherwise, it is a favorable prognostic gene" — The human cancer proteome - Methods summary - The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/cancer/method)
