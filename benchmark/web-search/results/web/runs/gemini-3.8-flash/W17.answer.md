**Q:** In the cancer section, what p-value must the Kaplan-Meier analysis reach for a gene to be considered prognostic, and what distinguishes an unfavorable prognostic gene from a favorable one?

**gemini-3.8-flash:**

Based on the Human Protein Atlas (**The human cancer proteome - Methods summary**: [https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/cancer/method](https://www.proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/cancer/method)):

* **Kaplan-Meier p-value threshold:** 
  A gene is defined as prognostic if it achieves a log-rank **$p\text{-value} < 0.001$** in maximally separated Kaplan-Meier analysis (provided its median expression is not $< 1\text{ pTPM}$, which would otherwise classify it as unprognostic due to low expression).

* **Unfavorable vs. favorable prognostic gene:** 
  * An **unfavorable prognostic gene** is one where the patient group with high expression of the gene has a higher number of observed events than expected events (i.e., higher expression is associated with poorer survival).
  * A **favorable prognostic gene** is one where the group with high expression does not have higher observed than expected events (i.e., higher expression is associated with better survival).
