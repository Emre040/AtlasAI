Subcellular

The subcellular resource now covers the subcellular localization of proteins encoded by 13603 genes (77 added and 8 removed), using a total of 15995 antibodies (134 added and 50 removed).

-
Blood

The blood resource has been expanded with over 2,500 samples profiled using two complementary plasma proteomics technologies: the Olink Explore HT and the SomaScan 11K platforms, measuring over 5,400 and 9,500 proteins, respectively. The samples represent 30 cohorts, including healthy individuals as well as patients with cardiovascular, metabolic, cancer, psychiatric, neurologic, autoimmune, and infectious diseases. This cross-platform, pan-disease dataset complements the previously released data generated with the Olink Explore 1536 platform, enabling exploration of pan-disease profiles for thousands of proteins and investigation of how protein levels relate to age, sex, and BMI across platforms. Additionally, the Olink HT longitudinal dataset, following children from early life into adulthood, has been complemented with SomaScan data. Complementing these datasets, we applied targeted proteomics with qRePS internal standards to the pregnancy cohort also quantified by Olink HT and SomaScan 11k, absolutely quantifying >100 plasma proteins in molar units (pmol/µl).

-
Structure

The Structure resource presents the in-house generated AlphaFold 2 predicted three-dimensional structures of 83795 protein isoforms for 19904 human genes with options to display different protein features as well as missense data from Ensembl Variant and AlphaMissense.

-
Interaction

The Interaction resource has expanded and now includes in-house generated AlphaFold 3 predicted three-dimensional structures for 22979 Consensus network interactions for 7855 genes in addition to interaction networks integrated with protein expression, location and classification data for 15216 genes.

Protein Atlas version 24.1
Release date: 2025.10.09

Ensembl version: 109

- The blood resource has been updated in connection with the first release of A human pan-disease blood atlas of the circulating proteome (Álvez et al., Science). The previous dataset, generated using the Olink Explore 1536 platform and encompassing over 6.000 patients across 59 diseases, is now complemented by newly generated data with the Olink Explore HT platform. This update introduces three new datasets: a pan-cancer cohort (including lung, colorectal, prostate, ovarian, and breast cancer), a longitudinal study of healthy adults, and a longitudinal study following children from early life into adulthood. Together, these datasets extend the existing pan-disease proteome profiles by enabling exploration of plasma proteome variability across the human lifespan, insights into protein detectability in plassma of healthy individuals, and extended profiles in five major cancer types. Additionally, we complement previous differential expression analyses performed for each disease type with results from machine learning classification models.

Protein Atlas version 24.0
Release date: 2024.10.22

Ensembl version: 109

- Twenty-fourth major release of the Human Protein Atlas with over 27,800 antibodies, targeting proteins from over 17,300 human genes (~87% of the human protein-coding genes). 8 separate resources with data covering various aspects of human protein-coding genes.

-
Tissue & Cancer

In total 3931 proteins are validated with Enhanced validation strategies. 7 antibodies were omitted. 44 new antibodies were added.

-
Tissue

The Tissue resource has launched a major update of multiplex tissue profiling. In total, 7 different antibody panels were used to study 610 proteins in testis, 178 proteins in ciliated cells, 162 proteins in kidney and 121 proteins in salivary gland based on overlap of antibody staining with cell/state-specific markers.

-
Cancer

To strengthen the reliability of the cancer resource, data from 10 independent cancer cohorts were integrated, creating a cross-validated, reliable collection of prognostic genes.
Furthermore, mass spectrometry datasets from CPTAC with cancerous and matched normal tissues across 11 cancer types has been incorporated.

-
Brain

The first high-resolution spatial transcriptomics data of the human cerebral cortex has been added. With a capture resolution of 0.5 micrometer, a both cellular and spatial information on protein expression is provided for the first time, using tools to quantify, impute and visualize protein expression in the human cerebral cortex.

-
Single Cell

The new Single Cell Resource consolidates the previously separate sections (Single Cell Type, Tissue Cell Type, and Immune Cell) into a unified structure. Additionally, it now includes Single Nuclei Brain data, representing expression profiles of 2.5 million brain cells. This resource offers a comprehensive single-cell overview of all protein-coding genes. The Tissue Cell Type dataset has been expanded to include new cell type enrichment data for the spleen, salivary glands, adrenal glands, and pituitary gland. Furthermore, the resource now incorporates external cluster data from the Tabula Sapiens project, allowing users to compare their findings with the clustering and cell annotation provided by the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) pipeline. With the inclusion of the brain single nuclei data, users can explore cell clusters across 11 distinct brain regions and compare these results with the cell type specificity across other datasets within the resource.

-
Subcellular

The Subcellular resource has in this version launched a major update in terms of a Cilia Atlas. The proteome and its subcellular organization have been systematically mapped in primary cilia across different cell lines, and in the flagellum of human sperm. With the help of high-throughput immunofluorescence imaging, we have been able to identify hundreds of proteins localized to these compartments, and detail their subcompartment localization.

-
Blood

This new resource combines the data from the former Blood and Disease sections together with a lot of extensive new information on blood profiles in patients with various diseases.
